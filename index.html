The rebate is worth far more than 5% of the average loss (about $106), because it pays only on losing trips, and those losses are large. Optimal playing plan, and why

Assumptions: a $700,000 bankroll, 0.924% cashback, a 5% rebate on each trip's net loss, a $3,000 bonus every trip, and 10,000 hands per 2-day trip at $50 or less. Single-line machines take 5 coins, so the realistic bets are $25 (a $5 machine) or $50 (a $10 machine).

$50Bet per hand at $700k, if strategy errors and the tax hit are small. Drop to $25 below about $480k.
$25Bet per hand instead if you lose about 0.1% to errors and owe about 35% tax under the 90% loss rule.
All handsEvery hand is worth at least +$0.25 at $50, whether you're ahead or behind. No stop-win and no stop-loss.
More tripsThe $3,000 bonus is 46% of profit at $50 and carries no risk. Another trip beats a bigger bet.

1. Bet size: why $50 is full Kelly, not half

Kelly sizing: bet per hand = bankroll × edge ÷ variance. The edge that goes in is only the part that grows when you bet more:

Part of the edgePer handGrows with the bet?
Cashback+0.924%Yes
House edge–0.422%Yes
5% loss rebate+0.209%Yes
Edge for Kelly+0.711%
$3,000 bonus0.60% at $50No. It's $3,000 at any bet size.

$700,000 × 0.711% ÷ 98.3 = $50.6 a hand. The exact bankroll-growth simulation peaks at about $55, and $50 gets more than 99% of that peak. If you put the whole 1.42% into the formula, you get $101, which is where "$50 is half Kelly" came from. That's wrong, because doubling the bet doesn't double the bonus. Above $50 your trips also get shorter (5,000 hands at $100), so the amount wagered stays at $500,000 and cashback stops growing. Going from $50 to $100 adds only about $400 of profit a trip, and the variance doubles.

A higher edge % doesn't mean faster growth. $25 and $100 both have higher edges than $50, and both grow the bankroll more slowly. Kelly weighs dollars against risk, not percentages.

Why full Kelly is safer here than the textbook says. In the textbook setup with no bonus, full Kelly has about a 50% chance of ever halving the bankroll, and half Kelly about 12.5%. The simulation reproduces both (48.8% and 11.9%). Your $3,000 bonus adds 0.43% of growth a trip with no risk, about two-thirds of your total. So at $50, the chance of ever halving over 1,000 trips is only about 10% if you resize bets with the bankroll, or about 13% at a fixed $50.

2. $25 or $50: what can flip the choice

The bet that maximizes growth with no bet-size limits ("Kelly bet") falls as anything drains the part of the edge that grows with the bet. Strategy errors do that, and so do taxes. Here's how each adjustment moves the choice at $700k, with 10,000 hands a trip:

  • The bonus never changes the bet. Cutting or dropping it lowers profit and growth, but the Kelly bet stays at $55. Casinos often reduce offers for winning players. Your comps (about $8,600 a trip) are far larger than the casino's expected win (about $2,100), so plan for the bonus possibly shrinking.
  • Strategy errors. The drill's "EV lost" is in bets per hand, the same unit as these error rates. Drill hands are deliberately hard, though, so your real-play rate is far lower than your drill average. Real-play rate ≈ total EV lost in real play ÷ hands played. Keeping it under 0.05% keeps $50 clearly ahead.
  • Taxes (US, from 2026). Only 90% of gambling losses are deductible, and only up to winnings. That makes 10% of every losing session's loss taxable, even when you broke even overall. The cost grows with the bet, so it lowers the Kelly bet. At a 35% rate with daily sessions, it costs about $1,000 a trip at $50 and about $500 at $25. If you take the standard deduction instead of itemizing, losses aren't deductible at all, and this analysis doesn't hold. Have a tax professional confirm how your sessions are counted.

3. Hand pays and time

From 2026, a jackpot of $2,000 or more needs a W-2G and a hand pay, and the machine locks until an attendant pays it. At $50 that's every quad, straight flush and royal. At $25 it's only the royal and the 80×-and-up quads.

  • The bankroll falls below the switch point: move to the $5 machine at $25. Switching early costs little ($25 keeps about 89% of the growth), and switching late risks deep drawdowns.
  • The bankroll grows: stay at $50. A $125 bet ($25 machine) wouldn't fit 10,000 hands, and with wagering capped at $500k a trip, extra bankroll buys safety, not growth, until about $2.9M.
  • $100 × 5,000 hands is about 1.7× Kelly at $700k. If you play it anyway, the exact solve gives stops of about +$55k / –$76k at the start, tightening to +$20k / –$62k after 4,000 hands. It still grows the bankroll more slowly than $50 played out.

6. Still to confirm with the casino

  • Rebate terms: whether it's figured before or after cashback, any cap or minimum loss, per trip or per day, and any play-through on the free play. The table above shows what each one does.
  • The bonus: what play it requires, and whether the offer survives a run of winning trips.
  • Hold strategy: perfect strategy is worth only about $8 a trip more than basic strategy at $50. The expensive errors are TDB's kicker holds and pat-hand vs. low-trips decisions. Practice those in the drill below.
