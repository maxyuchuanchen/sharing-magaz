The concentration, quantified

Royal flush, aces with a 2/3/4 kicker, and quad 2/3/4 with a low kicker are not where most of the return lives — the five common hands (jacks-or-better through full house) still account for roughly two-thirds of RTP. But relative to how rarely they land, those three hands are wildly overrepresented in the payback.

The headline number

Royal flush, aces-with-kicker, and quad-234-with-kicker occur on just 0.0265% of hands — about 1 in 3,774 deals — combined. Yet together they deliver 14.36% of the entire 99.58% return. That's a ≈539× overrepresentation: a dollar of payback from this slice is clustered into events roughly 539 times more concentrated than their occurrence rate alone would predict.

Widen the lens to the full bonus family — every quad category plus straight flush and royal — and the pattern holds at smaller scale: 0.244% of hands produce 27.9% of RTP, a ≈114× concentration. This is the defining trait of the Bonus-poker lineage: expected value is carried by frequent small hands, but realized return — and bankroll variance — is carried by a rare, high-pay tail. Sessions can run far below 99.58% for thousands of hands purely from missing that tail, independent of strategy quality.

What this means at the table

At $50/hand the per-hand result swings by $495 SD against a 71¢ mean — a 700:1 noise-to-signal ratio on any single hand. That ratio shrinks only with √hands, which is why even a full week of 5,000-hand days (35,000 hands) leaves a 95% range running from a six-figure loss to a six-figure win, and only a 60.6% chance of being ahead at all. The edge is real, but it is a long-run property: it takes on the order of 486,500 hands — about 97 days at this pace — before expected profit catches up to a single standard deviation of variance. The same kicker-quad tail that drives the RTP concentration above is exactly what drives this: the variance is dominated by the same rare, high-pay hands, not by the common ones.

Cross-check: a $50 bet as true half-Kelly at a 1.42% edge and this payout variance implies a bankroll near $690,600. If your actual roll is smaller than that, $50/hand is more aggressive than half-Kelly and the swings above understate your real risk of ruin; if it's larger, there's room to size up without leaving the half-Kelly band.
