The five common hands (jacks or better through full house) still pay about two-thirds of the return. The rare tail is what decides how a session actually goes. You can run well below 99.58% for thousands of hands just by missing it, even with perfect strategy.

Each $50 hand swings by $495 (1 SD) against an expected win of 71¢, about 700 to 1. The gap shrinks only with the square root of the number of hands. After a full week of 5,000-hand days you can still be anywhere from a six-figure loss to a six-figure win, with only a 60.6% chance of being ahead. It takes about 486,500 hands, roughly 97 days at this pace, before expected profit catches up to one standard deviation. The same rare kicker quads that concentrate the return also drive this variance.

A bankroll smaller than about $690,600 makes $50 a hand more aggressive than half-Kelly, so your real risk of ruin is higher than these ranges suggest. A larger bankroll leaves room to bet more and still stay within half-Kelly.
